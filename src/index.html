Welcome to open-lux

open-lux measures the light in your room with a small sensor you build yourself, and keeps your monitors as bright as the room needs – no brighter. This takes about ten minutes, once.

The two things ahead

  1. Build the sensor and plug it in. It is one resistor and one light-sensitive part on an Arduino – no soldering needed if you have a breadboard.
  2. Calibrate each monitor. open-lux measures what your panel actually emits, so it can match monitors to each other.

Nothing here changes your brightness yet. open-lux never touches a display it has not measured – that is deliberate, so a half-finished setup can never leave a screen stuck dark.

Build the sensor

open-lux cannot see your room without it. If you already have one built, just plug it in – this step notices on its own.

What you need

  • An Arduino Nano – an Uno, ESP8266 or ESP32 works just as well. A Nano is the cheapest and easiest to hide behind a monitor.
  • An LDR (light-dependent resistor), type GL5506. It looks like a small disc with a squiggle on top and costs pennies.
  • A 5.1kΩ resistor – anything from 4.7k to 10k is fine.
  • A USB cable that fits the Arduino, and a breadboard or three jumper wires.

Wire it up

  1. Connect one leg of the LDR to 5V.
  2. Connect the LDR's other leg to one leg of the resistor. This joint is the middle of the divider.
  3. Connect the resistor's free leg to GND.
  4. Run a wire from that middle joint to the analog pin A7.

The LDR has no polarity, so neither leg is the wrong way round.

Flash the Arduino

  1. Install the Arduino IDE from arduino.cc and plug the board into your PC with the USB cable.
  2. Open firmware/firmware.ino from the open-lux download.
  3. If you used a pin other than A7, change the line SENSOR_PIN = A7 to match.
  4. Pick your board and its port under Tools, then press Upload.

The firmware only reports raw light readings – everything else happens on your PC, so you never have to flash it again.

Calibrate your display

open-lux is about to sweep this monitor through its whole range and measure the light coming off it. It takes about a minute.

  1. Drag the open-lux window onto this display. Not sure which monitor that is? Press Identify below and it will blink.
  2. Turn the lights off. The sensor sees the room as well as the screen, so calibrate in the dark – a lit room is the single biggest source of a bad result.
  3. Hold the LDR flat against the square below, covering it.
  4. Press Calibrate, then leave everything alone – don't move the sensor and don't turn a light on until it finishes.

All set

From here

  • Home shows the room's light level and a slider. Nudge it whenever a room looks wrong – open-lux learns from it.
  • Settings holds the response curve, the theme, and the sensor's constants if you built yours with different parts. Calibrate is at the bottom, for any monitor you skipped or one you have moved.
